WHY NO LAUNCH WHISTLE

Nothing rises on screen. The shells appear where they burst, so a whistle would be scoring a rocket that was never drawn — and the ear catches that before the eye does. If a whistle is ever wanted, the picture has to earn it first.

WHY THE CRACKLE IS SPARSE

A crackle is not noise; it is a few hundred separate little reports. The buffer is filled at p = 0.004. The obvious way — dense noise with a decay on it — is what it was written as first, and it sounds like escaping steam. Press K to hear the mistake.

WHAT THIS COSTS THE GAME

Nothing. The sound is director’s-cut only: burst() is a bare function whose one call sits behind __DIRECTOR__, so closure deletes it from the 13312-byte build entirely. It is deliberately not a member of the SFX object — a property on that literal is reachable, so it could not be dropped and every byte would ship.

THE PICTURE IS THE HALF THAT PAYS

Shipping the fireworks at all costs the budget real bytes, and the effect here is the golfed one: 2px squares relying on the canvas fade for their trail, rather than the cut’s six-position stroked comets. That trade is what made it nearly fit.
